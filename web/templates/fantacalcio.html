Your squad, re-solved right now

Read the line under each name, not the Max column. "Bid up to 105, at 106 walk away" is the answer to they said one more, do I go again? — and it is exact, not a rule of thumb: his price is raised one credit at a time and the entire squad re-solved until the plan stops wanting him. The last price it still wants him at is your ceiling. Go one over and you are, measurably, better off losing him and spending the money on the next name in the chain.

Buy is what I think he goes for. Max is what the k-th best player at his role costs in this market — a price ladder, not a worth ceiling, so the best player at each role always shows max ≈ buy by construction. Do not read that as "no value there"; that column is context, the walk-away is the decision. Need is what you lose if a rival takes him, after accounting for the credits that frees up and who is still on the shelf — a low Need on an expensive player means let him go. The fallback chain is cumulative: it names who you buy if he goes, then who you buy if that one goes too, with each of their own ceilings measured in the state you would actually be bidding in.

Everything here is recomputed against the live board. Mark players bought or gone and the ceilings move — lose a substitute and the man you still want gets more irreplaceable, and his walk-away rises. A number with ≈ is a one-solve estimate shaded deliberately low; it firms up to the exact figure a moment later.

Your league runs the modificatore di difesa, so the base 6.0 stops cancelling for defenders and the keeper: their raw voto is currency, not just their bonus. Measured, though, it does not change how you bid. The modifier is worth about 47 points a season and you collect nearly all of it with any competent back four — pushing spend from 142 to 190 credits in defence bought 13 more modifier points and lost 26 overall. It is a windfall, not a strategy. Its one real effect on this board: Pavlovic (19) displaces Spinazzola on vote quality, and the fourth defender is now a genuine starter rather than a half-slot, because the whole bonus is forfeited if you cannot field four.

What this board does not know

The window shuts the same day you draft. The Italian market closes 1 September and the auction is the night of 1–2 September. Six listone players had already left when this was built; anyone sold on deadline day is not in here. Re-run the builder that afternoon.

{{ board.counts.blind }} of {{ board.counts.listone }} players have no usable record anywhere. A further {{ board.counts.foreign }} have none in Serie A but a real one abroad — those are priced on their Premier League / La Liga / Bundesliga / Ligue 1 record, converted at factors measured on the 164 players who made the same move since 2017 (goals ×0.93, assists ×1.00, minutes at 85%), a method that beat the market-value fallback on 4 of 6 historical debutant classes. The truly blind rows come from a regression on age, market value and role whose R² is {{ '%.2f'|format(board.validation.blind_fit.C.r2_bonus) }}–{{ '%.2f'|format(board.validation.blind_fit.P.r2_bonus) }} for bonus — close to no signal — so they are capped at replacement level: a bench slot at 1 credit, never upside. They are tagged no data. Do not read a max bid on a blind row as a recommendation.

Injuries are a snapshot, not a feed. Projected minutes already subtract the known absences from the latest Transfermarkt bulletin (shown in red under the player), but anyone hurt after that snapshot is priced as fit. Skim the news the afternoon of the auction.

Media voto is a property of the defence, not just the defender. A centre-back who spent last season in a leaky side carries a voto that understates him, and the summer signings are exactly who you will be bidding against other people for. The modifier numbers here do not re-centre a transferred player on his new team's defensive quality, so treat every player marked new in defence as under-rated by this board rather than genuinely cheap.

The band table is set by your league admin. This board uses the common three-band table (+1 at 6.00, +3 at 6.50, +6 at 7.00). Running it under the six-band table instead changes the modifier's value a lot (47 → 101 points) but returns the same back four, so the ambiguity does not change a single bid.

It is backtested on {{ board.validation.auction_n }} past auctions, and it wins {{ board.validation.auction_wins }} of them. Each replay uses only what was known before that auction, buys a squad at prices the room would have set, and is scored on what those players actually did — median {{ board.validation.auction_edge_median_pct }}% more realised bonus than buying by last season's points, capturing {{ board.validation.auction_capture_pct }}% of what perfect foresight would have got. Ranking accuracy is a narrower win: Spearman {{ board.validation.spearman_model }} against {{ board.validation.spearman_naive }} for "last season repeats", averaged over eight seasons. Most of the edge comes from buying the right players at the wrong price, not from forecasting them far better.

Minutes are the dominant error, not scoring rate. A player whose role changes in September breaks the projection in a way no amount of xG modelling fixes — which is why the backtest counts players who vanished as zero rather than dropping them. Refitting the scoring weights to fantacalcio's own scoring was tested and made things worse in five of six seasons, so the 3-per-goal / 1-per-assist rules stand as written.

Keepers who just became first choice are under-priced by me, not by the market. The model reads historical minutes, so a new number one looks like a backup. Where the market pays far more than my max for a keeper, the market is probably right and I am probably stale.

Bonus is the currency, not the vote. With 25-man rosters in a 10-team league your bench supplies the 6.0 base vote too, so for outfielders it cancels out and only goals, assists and cards separate players. It does not cancel in goal, where one keeper plays — so keepers are valued on goals prevented against a replacement keeper.
